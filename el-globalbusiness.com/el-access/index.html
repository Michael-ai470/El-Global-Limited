      How the saving works

      What’s an Empty Leg?

      When someone charters a jet one way, the aircraft still has to fly home, or on to its next pickup. Those repositioning flights are empty legs.

      Rather than fly them empty, operators sell them for a fraction of the charter price. You get the same aircraft, the same crew and the same service, on the operator’s timing.

      The trade-off is flexibility: the route and the departure window come from the original trip, and can occasionally move.

      Questions

      Good to Know

      Anything else, ask us directly: send a message.

      Why are empty legs so much cheaper?

      The aircraft has to make the trip anyway to reposition. Anything the operator earns on it covers costs they would otherwise carry, so they price it far below a normal charter. The aircraft, crew and service are exactly the same.

      What if my flight is cancelled or moved?

      An empty leg depends on the original booking, so its timing can occasionally change. If the operator cancels, you receive a full refund, and we look for an alternative flight with you straight away.

      Are these flights safe?

      Every flight is operated by a licensed charter operator: FAA Part 135 in the United States, EASA or the local equivalent elsewhere. The operator is named on your confirmation.

      How many people can fly?

      The whole aircraft is yours, up to its seats: from 7 on a light jet to 16 on the largest. Split between friends or colleagues, an empty leg often costs less per person than business class.

      Can I bring luggage or pets?

      Luggage space depends on the aircraft, and we confirm it with your booking. Pets are welcome on most flights; mention them in your request.

      Can I book for a company or a group?

      Yes. Send a request for any flight, or tell us the routes you fly often and we will match you with empty legs as they appear.
